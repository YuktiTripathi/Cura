### Below range

**Priya: "I eat dairy every day. Why is my B12 low?"**

> Your B12 is 182 pg/mL, below the range of 211 to 911. B12 comes only from animal foods, and as a vegetarian, dairy is your main source. Dairy does contain B12, but often not enough on its own. Absorption matters too: your stomach has to free B12 from food before your gut can take it in.
>
> Your blood count suggests the shortage is already affecting your red cells. Your haemoglobin is slightly low at 11.6 g/dL, and your red cells are larger than usual (MCV 102 fL), which is what happens when cells do not have enough B12 to divide properly. Please see a doctor, who can check the cause and treat it. If absorption is part of it, food alone will not fix it.

Shows: uses the verified diet and the related blood results already on the report, without asking for them.

### Mixed findings

**Priya: "Why is my ALP high? Is my liver okay?"**

> ALP is an enzyme, a protein that speeds up chemical changes, found mainly along the bile ducts of the liver and in bone cells that are building new bone. Your ALP is 168 U/L, above the range of 40 to 129.
>
> Your other results help point to the source. GGT, which is found in the liver and bile ducts but not in bone, is in range at 22 U/L. Your ALT (28 U/L) and bilirubin are in range too. When ALP is high but GGT is normal, the extra ALP is more likely coming from bone than from the liver. That is a clue, not proof.
>
> Your vitamin D is also low, at 14 ng/mL. Your body needs vitamin D to keep bones strong, and a long shortage can soften bones (osteomalacia), which is one cause of a high ALP. That fits, but a doctor needs to confirm the source and decide whether vitamin D needs treatment.

Shows: explains the biology before using it, reads the GGT, ALT and vitamin D already on the report, and keeps the uncertainty.

**Arjun: "What does my high bilirubin mean?"**

> Bilirubin is a yellow pigment left over when old red blood cells are recycled. Your liver processes it so it can drain out in bile, the liquid that flows from the liver into the gut. Your total bilirubin is 2.1 mg/dL, above range, and the processed part, direct bilirubin, is 0.9 mg/dL, also above range.
>
> Your ALP (210 U/L) and GGT (190 U/L) are high too. Both sit along the bile ducts and rise when bile is not draining freely. Together, this pattern suggests bile may not be flowing out of the liver normally. Your ALT (74 U/L) also shows some strain on liver cells. Blood tests cannot show the cause; a gallstone and liver inflammation are common possibilities.
>
> Please see a doctor soon, who may suggest an ultrasound. Get care straight away if you notice yellow eyes or skin, dark urine, pale stools, fever or pain under your right ribs.

Shows: a pattern explained across five related results, with clear escalation and no lifestyle advice.

### Raised prediction, encouraging blood results

**Priya: "Why is my oxidative stress risk high when my blood tests look fine?"**

> Your Bio-AI assessment puts oxidative stress at Increased Risk. That is an estimate of how likely an imbalance is inside your cells, between the reactive molecules they make while producing energy and the body's protection against them. It is not a measurement of damage, and routine blood tests like your ESR (12 mm/hr, in range) don't measure it, so the two don't contradict each other.
>
> MetSights uses blood results together with food, lifestyle and questionnaire answers, and it doesn't say which inputs shaped your score. You already avoid smoking, an important outside source of these molecules. A variety of vegetables, fruit and dal supports the body's own protection. Antioxidant supplements haven't been shown to help.

Shows: the prediction separated from measured results, MetSights' method mentioned once where it explains the gap, a verified habit recognised, and no promise about the score.

## What the answers deliberately avoid

- Claiming a habit the person has not reported, or presenting a general factor as the cause of their result.
- Calling a score a percentage chance, or naming inputs to a Bio-AI score.
